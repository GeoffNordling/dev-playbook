# Writing Problems

The catalog of recurring problems in Claude's document writing. Each
entry names a pattern a review can cite to reject a draft. A problem
enters the catalog when it recurs; a countermeasure, once built, is
linked from its entry.

- **Slop tics** — sentence-level style patterns: changelog residue,
  contrast pairs, closing cadence, and the rest. The named tics live in
  [slop-tics.md](/standards/prose/slop-tics.md); /document-deslop
  dispatches the deslopper agent to remove them from a document.
- **External-doc pointing** — the draft points readers at official
  documentation for features it does not cover.
- **Enforcement meta-commentary** — the draft declares itself binding
  or names the lint that enforces it.
- **Relationship meta-commentary** — the draft describes its
  relationship to other machinery — craft skills, sibling processes —
  instead of stating its rules.
- **Duplication instead of integration** — the draft restates a fact
  another document owns instead of citing that document or moving the
  fact to its owner.
- **Section-scale duplication** — the draft carries a whole section
  that near-verbatim duplicates another standard, instead of landing
  the section in that standard.
- **Stale-prone examples** — the draft cites specific corpus files as
  examples; the examples go stale as the corpus moves. Name the rules
  and the classes they govern.
- **Nonexistent-thing mentions** — the draft mentions things decided
  not to exist.
- **Unapproved negative examples** — the draft teaches what not to do
  without a specific reason or user approval.
- **Coined transitions** — the draft invents transitional phrases the
  reader must decode.
- **Platform explainers** — the draft explains how the platform
  operates where its job is to state how we write.
- **Dropped rulings** — a ruling issued against one document is not
  carried to the next; every rule is re-litigated per file. The
  meta-problem: it multiplies every other entry.
